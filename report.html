Beyond the three-spinor gate limitation

Substantial change: a coupled Gaussian six-spinor candidate now yields a heralded approximation to an infinite-order phase gate, using the previously recorded A filter. Its algebra and a 25-dimensional replay pass. The unresolved step is matching this representation, code and charge projectors to the audited physical six-spinor AFT/DAZ data. This is a conditional construction, not a completed physical gate theorem.

1. What the existing results actually constrain

The current so5_braid_image_dispatch_record.md records finite projective B₃ images of order 60 in both checked lifts and spinor sectors. The resource-ancilla note records 32/32 checks but uses a product of two three-spinor blocks; it explicitly leaves cross-triple recoupling open. Those results remain intact. Neither is a global no-go for coupled six-spinor protocols.

There is an all-depth structural observation: when every branch remains separable, its operator is Lᵦ ⊗ Rᵦ. Preparing and reading an independent ancilla contracts Rᵦ to a scalar, leaving cᵦLᵦ. Thus separable ancillas cannot enlarge the logical-only instrument closure, even with adaptive finite histories. This does not prove that deeper logical-only filters can never generate gates; it identifies exactly why the ancilla must interact with the data.

3. A verified four-spinor resource benchmark

Cui–Wang's SO(5)₂ sector Hom(y₁,s⁴) has dimension five. Its basis is (y₂y₂, 1y₁, y₂y₁, y₁y₂, y₁1). The published B₄ matrices generate the single-qupit Clifford image and give explicit Fourier, X and Z words; their SO(5)₂ universality proposal remains partial. This replay verifies those identities exactly in Q(ζ₅) and independently enumerates 3,000 projective matrices numerically. The enumeration is not an exact group-order certificate.

Assume a known basis state can be initialized and the instrument coherently distinguishes vacuum from its complement on the first spinor pair. Prepare the uniform five-state superposition. Vacuum is the single basis position 1. Accepting non-vacuum and applying X⁻¹ gives

|M₅⟩ = (|1⟩ + |2⟩ + |3⟩ + |4⟩)/2,
P(accept) = 4/5,   expected fresh preparations = 5/4.
W(q,p) = (1/5) Σᵧ ζ₅^(2py) M₅(q−y) M₅(q+y)*.
W(0,p) = −1/20 for p ≠ 0,
Σ |W| = 1 + 2/√5,   total negative mass = 1/√5.

The displayed negativity is exact. A single-qupit stabilizer state either has one nonzero computational amplitude or has five equal-magnitude amplitudes; this four-support state is therefore outside the stabilizer states. Numerical enumeration of all 30 pure stabilizer states gives maximum fidelity 4/5. This is a resource witness, not a gate-injection or distillability theorem.

Measurement distinction: coherent non-vacuum projection is the channel QρQ. Fully resolving y₁ and y₂ and deleting the record implements P₁ρP₁ + P₂ρP₂. For the uniform input, the latter accepted state has only 1/2 fidelity with the coherent target. An off-diagonal coherence connecting the two charge groups distinguishes the instruments directly.

The obvious one-copy injection fails. SUM with computational ancilla readout gives diagonal Kraus entries M₅(b−j), hence rank four on every branch. Even Clifford-pre-rotating the resource does not fix it: exact overlap checks show none of the six stabilizer bases is flat on M₅. Thus this specific one-copy SUM/readout family has no full five-dimensional unitary branch. Other encodings and injection circuits remain open.

4. The coupled six-spinor candidate

The fusion rule calculation is exact:

s³ = 3s + 2s′,
dim Hom(1,s⁶) = 3² + 2² = 13.
Triple-split sectors: (s,s) has 9 states; (s′,s′) has 4.
Adjacent-pair projectors (1,y₁,y₂) have ranks (3,5,5).

The four extra states cannot be dropped during cross-triple braiding. A rank-three vacuum branch also cannot carry a nonzero unitary on an unknown four-dimensional two-qubit code; that rank obstruction does not exclude one unknown qubit plus a fixed ancilla.

Use a Gaussian model on C⁵ ⊗ C⁵ with simultaneous inversion (x,y) ↦ (−x,−y). Its even sector has dimension 13. Let D|x⟩ = ζ₅^(x²)|x⟩ and T = F D⁻¹F†. The five generators are D₁, T₁, G₃, T₂, D₂, where G₃|x,y⟩ = ζ₅^((x+y)²)|x,y⟩. Exact local and shifted 5×5 identities prove the Artin relations; far commutation is immediate. This is a coupled braid representation, rather than a product of B₃ actions.

Let eⱼ = (|j⟩ + |−j⟩)/√2, j = 1,2. Encode data in span(e₁,e₂), prepare the ancilla (e₁−e₂)/√2, apply G₃, and project back to the locally even triple sector. The local recoupled vacuum vector (1,√2,√2)/√5 gives exactly the recorded phi-filter A = [[3,−2],[−2,3]]/5. This is a useful local compatibility check, but not a full six-spinor gauge identification.

An ideal same-line ancilla return has Kraus operator

K = diag(√5 − ζ₅, √5 − ζ₅⁻¹)/4,
K†K = p I,        p = (7 + √5)/32 ≈ 0.2886271243.
λ = (√5 − ζ₅⁻¹)/(√5 − ζ₅).

Here λ lies in Q(ζ₅) but equals none of its ten roots of unity ±ζ₅ʲ. The resulting normalized gate therefore has infinite projective order. This exact algebraic fact would put it outside any finite braid stabilizer. It establishes neither a universal gate set nor deterministic branch correction.

A finite circuit replaces the ideal return projector

Do not assume that preparing a resource line automatically supplies its projective measurement. Instead, apply the existing A filter n times on the ancilla and then consume/measure the ancilla, retaining the two phi outcomes as accepted. Because Aⁿ = P₋ + 5⁻ⁿP₊, the accepted data channel in this model is exactly

𝓔ₙ(ρ) = [p UρU† + 25⁻ⁿ q VρV†] / [p + 25⁻ⁿ q],
U = K/√p,   V = diag(1−ζ₅, −1+ζ₅⁻¹)/(4√q),
q = (5 − √5)/32.
P(accept) = p + 25⁻ⁿ q, independent of the unknown input.
||𝓔ₙ − U(·)U†||⋄ ≤ 2·25⁻ⁿ q / (p + 25⁻ⁿ q).

The wrong-unitary weight shrinks as 25⁻ⁿ. Numerical replay independently checks all branches for n = 0 through 5 against the algebraic formula. At n = 5 the diamond-norm upper bound is about 6.13×10⁻⁸, while success remains about 28.86%. This is ideal-model approximation error; it excludes physical operation noise and preparation cost. It is a heralded protocol: rejected charge/filter outcomes are not repaired here.

Remaining theorem obligation: identify this Gaussian module, its e₁/e₂ basis, the locally even triple projector, and the cross generator with the same audited physical SO(5)₂ F/R lift. A braid relation and matching local filter alone do not establish that identification. Until then, this is a verified coupled-model candidate, not an integrated physical gate result.

5. The next concrete test and its pass condition

  1. Build the complete 13-state vacuum fusion tree from the audited datum. Preserve both 9-state and 4-state triple sectors. Generate σ₁,…,σ₅ and the physical triple-charge projectors from that datum.
  2. Solve one simultaneous intertwiner against the Gaussian module, including projectors and code embeddings. Match braid phases consistently and verify all equations exactly; matching spectra or a single 3×3 block is insufficient. Repeat separately for the selected AFT and DAZ lifts and spinor labels.
  3. If the match succeeds, replay G₃, the s/s projection, Aⁿ and both ancilla outcomes in the physical fusion basis. Check Kraus completeness including rejection branches, absence of accepted logical leakage, input-independent total acceptance, and the two-unitary channel formula.
  4. A successful replay upgrades the candidate to a heralded approximate non-braid gate in that lift. Deterministic recovery, a second noncommuting logical gate, entangling operations and a universality proof remain separate tasks.
